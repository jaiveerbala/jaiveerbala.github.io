Mechanical · 2025
Differential Swerve Drivebase

A personal project done entirely solo. Differential gearing is a common principle in robotics — I applied it to a drivetrain where two motors per pod, acting through a differential, can independently control both wheel rotation speed and pod steering angle simultaneously, giving infinite combinations of motion with just two motors per side.

  • Designed entirely in SolidWorks CAD from scratch
  • Manufactured, assembled, and tested the full physical prototype
  • Used differential gearing: two motors per pod for independent wheel + steering control
  • Two pods total — four motors drive the entire robot with omnidirectional capability
  • 3D-printed prototype validated the concept; metal manufacturing cost prevented competition use

Mechanical · 2025
2-Speed Transmission

A servo-actuated 2-speed transmission designed in SolidWorks for FTC robotics. Switches between 1:1 and 5:3 gear ratios in under a second using a spring-loaded linear shift along a shaft. Particularly useful for endgame tasks where a mechanism needs to be fast during normal play but requires more torque at the end of the match.

  • Designed in SolidWorks CAD
  • Switches between 1:1 and 5:3 torque ratio in under one second
  • Spring-loaded linear shift actuated by a servo motor
  • Designed specifically for FTC endgame linear slide applications
Mechanical · 2025
Differential Wrist (ITD Claw)

A two-DOF wrist mechanism for the Into the Deep season robot. Uses a differential gearing system so that both the orbit angle and the claw rotation can be controlled simultaneously with cleaner wire routing and simpler kinematics than handling each DOF separately.

  • Designed in SolidWorks CAD
  • Differential gearing achieves simultaneous orbit and rotation of the claw
  • Enables picking up specimens off the field wall and repositioning for optimal scoring
  • Simpler wire management versus two independently driven DOFs

Embedded · Electrical · 2023 · 7th Grade
Acoustic Levitator

Standing waves between two opposing ultrasonic transducers create nodes of pressure that can suspend small objects in midair without any physical contact. Built by desoldering the transmitters from SR04 sensors and driving them with a motor controller for amplification.

  • Desoldered ultrasonic transmitters from SR04 sensors
  • Powered through a motor controller to amplify output — more than stock driver circuits provide
  • Required careful physical alignment and distance calibration to find the standing wave sweet spot
  • 3D-printed enclosure once a working alignment was found
  • Plug-in power cable on the back powers both the Arduino and motor controller
  • Can stack multiple pieces of foam into spinning rings of levitated balls
Embedded · 2025 · 8th Grade
Raspberry Pi CAD File Server

Built a self-hosted file server for the FTC robotics team to solve a real workflow problem: SolidWorks didn't offer free student PDM (file sharing) at the time, leaving the team without a reliable way to sync CAD files across members.

  • Raspberry Pi 3B+ running 24/7 in my room as a persistent sync node
  • Resilio Sync monitors the shared folder and pushes changes to all connected devices
  • Always-on server ensures the latest files are available even when all other devices are offline
  • Built as a workaround before SolidWorks offered free student PDM licenses

Software · 2023 · 7th Grade
Unbeatable Tic-Tac-Toe
✕ ○ ✕

A Python console Tic-Tac-Toe game against the computer. No machine learning or AI — just hardcoded logic, multi-step simulated lookaheads, fork detection and blocking. The result is a computer that essentially can't be beaten; most games end in a draw.

  • Python console-based, player vs. computer
  • Computer attempts to fork the player (create two simultaneous winning threats)
  • Computer detects and blocks player fork attempts early
  • Uses hardcoded cases, search algorithms, and multi-step simulated outcome trees — no AI
  • Essentially unbeatable — most games result in a tie; some in a computer win

A concept developed for an engineering challenge — a self-coordinating fleet of AI-powered drones that can autonomously clean large outdoor areas far faster than any human crew.
